Portugal has the pieces for a women’s health strategy. It doesn’t have the strategy.

November 22, 2026

Portugal does not have a national women’s health strategy. So I borrowed a scorecard built for countries that do, and graded what we have.

The scorecard is the Women’s Health System and Strategy Index from Thémis, a European women’s health fund and think tank. Their October 2026 report scores a country’s national strategy on 26 items in four groups: twelve health conditions, six system levers, two questions on how priorities are justified, and six governance items, each scored 1 to 3. The first phase covered Austria, France, Ireland, the Netherlands and Norway. Portugal was not included, because there is no strategy to score.

I wanted to know what Portugal would look like on that scale. So I went through the 26 items and scored each one against the closest public documents I could find: DGS norms, national programmes, the Plano Nacional de Saúde 2030, recent state budgets and ENIND, the national equality strategy. Every score links to the document it was graded against. The result: 51 out of 78, with three clear patterns.

What is genuinely strong

Maternal care and cancer screening score 3. Portugal has a national programme for low-risk pregnancy surveillance, a 2023 DGS norm organising care before, during and after pregnancy, and a quantified target in the national health plan: maternal mortality at or below 7.1 per 100,000 live births in 2028-2030. Breast and cervical cancer screening are structured, population-based programmes with current norms, alongside HPV vaccination.

What is promised but undelivered

Endometriosis and menopause score 2. Parliament recommended a national endometriosis strategy in 2023 and reinforced it in 2024, and no funded programme exists. Menopause entered the state budget for the first time in 2025, with a national strategy promised for the first half of 2026. That deadline was missed, the topic then disappeared from the 2027 budget proposal, parliament passed a resolution in October, and in November the government said new DGS programmes, menopause included, are in development. Movement, but not yet delivery.

What is not there at all

Cardiovascular, metabolic, neurological, musculoskeletal and autoimmune health all score 1: I found no women-specific government action in public sources. This is the part that stays with me. These are exactly the conditions where the data and research gaps decide what science gets done and whose symptoms get taken seriously. And there is no dedicated funding line for women’s health anywhere in the state budget.

The scorecard, item by item

Each item from the scorecard work, with its score, the current situation in Portugal and the sources it was graded against. Scores use the Thémis 1-3 scale; the method note at the end explains how to read them.

Health conditions · 21/36

1. Endometriosis 2/3

Parliament recommended a national strategy against endometriosis and adenomyosis (48/2023) and reinforced SNS support with specialised centres, medication reimbursement, awareness campaigns and professional training (111/2024). No funded government strategy or programme exists yet.

Scored against: Resoluções da Assembleia da República 48/2023 and 111/2024

Sources: RAR 48/2023 · RAR 111/2024

2. Reproductive & gynaecological 2/3

Lei 53/2025 expanded family-planning consultations into full sexual and reproductive health consultations with guaranteed free access, covering contraception, fertility and PMA. Abortion has been legal and provided in the SNS since 2007. No dedicated programme with targets.

Scored against: Lei 53/2025 and SNS sexual and reproductive health services

Sources: Lei 53/2025 · DGS: IVG legislation

3. Maternal 3/3

Portugal's strongest area. A named national programme for low-risk pregnancy surveillance (PNVGBR), a 2023 DGS norm organising preconception, pregnancy and puerperium care, and a quantified PNS 2030 target: maternal mortality at or below 7.1 per 100,000 live births in 2028-2030.

Scored against: DGS Norma 001/2023, PNVGBR and Plano Nacional de Saúde 2030

Sources: DGS Norma 001/2023 · DGS: PNVGBR · PNS 2030 (full PDF)

4. Mental health 2/3

A national mental health plan exists and DGS published a dedicated manual on promoting mental health in pregnancy and early infancy, with perinatal mental health flagged by the SNS. No women-specific targets or funded women's programme.

Scored against: Plano Nacional de Saúde Mental 2021-2030 and DGS perinatal mental health guidance

Sources: DGS: perinatal mental health manual · SNS: perinatal mental health

5. Menopause 2/3

Politically live but undelivered. Menopause first entered the state budget in 2025; OE2026 promised a national strategy in the first half of 2026, a deadline the government missed before the topic disappeared from OE2027. In October 2026 parliament approved a resolution (without force of law) for consultations, reimbursement and a DGS literacy programme, and in November 2026 the government said new DGS sexual and reproductive health programmes including menopause are in development.

Scored against: OE2026 commitment, October 2026 parliamentary resolution, November 2026 government announcement

Sources: JN, 16 Oct 2026 · Público, 7 Nov 2026 · HealthNews, 28 Oct 2026

6. Cancer 3/3

Named, structured population-based screening programmes with current norms: breast cancer (Norma 12/2024) and cervical cancer (Norma 09/2024), both women-specific with defined pathways, plus HPV vaccination.

Scored against: DGS population-based screening programmes

Sources: DGS Norma 12/2024 (breast) · DGS Norma 09/2024 (cervical)

7. Sexual health 2/3

A named national programme for STIs and HIV exists, and Lei 53/2025 guarantees free sexual and reproductive health consultations including STI prevention. Contraception, including emergency contraception, is free at SNS family-planning consultations.

Scored against: Programa Nacional IST/VIH and Lei 53/2025

Sources: DGS: PN IST/VIH · DGS: contraception

8. Cardiovascular 1/3

Portugal has a national cerebro-cardiovascular disease programme, but nothing in government policy addresses women's cardiovascular risk specifically. Women-focused activity is society-led, such as the Portuguese Society of Cardiology joining Go Red for Women.

Scored against: No women-specific government programme found

Sources: HealthNews, 6 Mar 2026 · News Farma: SPC Go Red for Women

9. Musculoskeletal 1/3

No women-specific national programme found in public sources. Osteoporosis and musculoskeletal disease are covered generically, despite their heavier burden on women.

Scored against: PNS 2030 (no women-specific action)

Sources: PNS 2030 (full PDF)

10. Metabolic 1/3

No women-specific national programme found. Gestational diabetes is addressed inside maternal care norms rather than as a women's health priority.

Scored against: PNS 2030 (no women-specific action)

Sources: PNS 2030 (full PDF)

11. Neurological & cognitive 1/3

No women-specific national programme found in public sources, despite women's higher burden of dementia and stroke.

Scored against: PNS 2030 (no women-specific action)

Sources: PNS 2030 (full PDF)

12. Autoimmune & inflammatory 1/3

No women-specific national programme found in public sources, although autoimmune conditions disproportionately affect women.

Scored against: PNS 2030 (no women-specific action)

Sources: PNS 2030 (full PDF)

System levers · 13/18

13. Research & innovation 2/3

FCT ran a funded Gender Research 4 Covid-19 programme integrating sex and gender analysis into research, showing the lever exists. There is no standing women's health research call or dedicated funding line.

Scored against: FCT funding programmes

Sources: FCT: Gender Research 4 Covid-19

14. Health care delivery 2/3

Universal SNS coverage; Lei 53/2025 commits to nationwide sexual and reproductive health consultations; a 2026 ministerial dispatch defines the hospital referral network for obstetrics and gynaecology. Menopause referral exists only in some local health units, with no dedicated new programme or budget.

Scored against: SNS structure, Lei 53/2025 and 2026 referral network

Sources: Lei 53/2025 · Obstetrics & gynaecology referral network (PDF)

15. Workforce education 2/3

DGS clinical norms orient professional practice (for example Norma 001/2023 on pregnancy care), and parliament recommended endometriosis training for health professionals. No dedicated, funded women's health training programme exists.

Scored against: DGS norms and parliamentary recommendations

Sources: DGS Norma 001/2023 · RAR 111/2024

16. Public awareness 2/3

Screening awareness campaigns run through DGS and partners, parliament recommended endometriosis awareness campaigns, and in November 2026 the government announced information materials on pre- and post-menopause for the public and health professionals, including SNS 24.

Scored against: DGS campaigns and November 2026 government announcement

Sources: Público, 7 Nov 2026 · RAR 111/2024

17. Legislation & policy 3/3

Legislation is an actively used lever for women's health in Portugal: Lei 53/2025 on sexual and reproductive health consultations, the 2007 abortion law, free contraception in the SNS, the 2022 VAT cut on menstrual products to 6%, and recent parliamentary resolutions on menopause and endometriosis.

Scored against: Enacted laws and fiscal policy

Sources: Lei 53/2025 · SIC: menstrual products VAT to 6% · DGS: contraception

18. Financing & reimbursement 2/3

Concrete entitlements exist: contraception (including emergency contraception) is free in the SNS, abortion and publicly funded PMA are covered, and screening is free. Fertility medication was announced to become free. Menopause reimbursement was proposed in parliament but not delivered.

Scored against: SNS entitlements and announced changes

Sources: DGS: contraception · ERS: IVG regime (PDF) · Fertility medication to become free

Justification of priorities · 4/6

19. Population health data 2/3

ENIND and PNS 2030 draw on EIGE indices and national statistics, and PNS 2030 includes a broad health-situation analysis. No formal burden-of-disease assessment of women's health was used to select priorities, the same gap Thémis found in all five strategies.

Scored against: ENIND 2018-2030 and PNS 2030

Sources: PNS 2030: health situation · RCM 61/2018 (ENIND)

20. Patient & public input 2/3

ENIND's action plans went through public consultation, a structured process but not a large-scale consultation of women or patients on health priorities. No women-health-specific engagement was found.

Scored against: ENIND action plan consultation

Sources: ENIND 2022-2025 public consultation draft (PDF)

Governance · 13/18

Scored against ENIND 2018-2030 "Portugal + Igual", the closest national strategy covering women. Portugal has no health strategy for these items to attach to.

21. Owner assigned 2/3

CIG (the equality commission) coordinates ENIND together with line ministries. No individual lead is named, and no body owns women's health specifically. The DGS director said in 2024 a national women's health programme was not yet necessary.

Scored against: ENIND 2018-2030

Sources: CIG: Portugal + Igual · HealthNews, 20 Jun 2024

22. Funding 1/3

No named budget exists for women's health measures. The OE2026 menopause commitment carried no allocation, and menopause was dropped from the OE2027 proposal.

Scored against: State budgets 2025-2027

Sources: HealthNews, 28 Oct 2026 · JN, 16 Oct 2026

23. Timeline 3/3

ENIND runs to 2030 with phased action plans (2018-2021 and 2022-2025), a named period with internal phasing.

Scored against: ENIND 2018-2030

Sources: RCM 61/2018 (ENIND)

24. Measurable process goals 2/3

Action plans list measures with responsible entities and execution rates, but health measures are few and framed around promoting a gender perspective in policy design rather than concrete delivery commitments.

Scored against: ENIND action plans

Sources: ENIND 2025 execution report (PDF)

25. Measurable outcome goals 2/3

ENIND goals are directional. PNS 2030 does set quantified targets, including maternal mortality at or below 7.1 per 100,000 live births in 2028-2030, but there are no women's-health-strategy-level outcome targets.

Scored against: ENIND and PNS 2030

Sources: PNS 2030 (full PDF)

26. Monitoring & evaluation 3/3

CIG publishes annual execution reports for ENIND, a named, recurring public accountability mechanism. The 2025 report was published in 2026.

Scored against: ENIND annual execution reports

Sources: ENIND 2025 execution report (PDF)

The pattern is the argument

Portugal is not starting from zero. Legislation is an actively used lever: free contraception in the public health service, publicly funded fertility treatment, a 2025 law creating full sexual and reproductive health consultations, the VAT cut on menstrual products. The pieces exist. What is missing is what connects them: a strategy with an owner, a budget line and outcome targets. In 2024 the director-general of health argued that a national women’s health programme was not necessary yet. Scoring the pieces against an international rubric points me to the opposite conclusion: the gap is not competence, it is coordination and commitment.

Method and sources

I want to be precise about what this is. It is a proxy gap analysis, not an official Index score. The Thémis rubric rewards action introduced by a women’s health strategy, which Portugal does not have, so I scored the existing policy landscape against the same 1-3 scale. A score of 1 means no women-specific national programme was found in the public sources I checked on 22 November 2026; local pilots, hospital-level services and civil society work may still exist. The six governance items are scored against ENIND 2018-2030, the equality strategy, because it is the closest national strategy covering women, and it is not a health one.

Methodology: Thémis, “Towards a Women’s Health System and Strategy Index”, October 2026 (wearethemis.org).
